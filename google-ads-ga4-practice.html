01Project overview

Most of my live campaign work is on Meta. Google Search and GA4 are where I'm building hands-on depth, so this page shows the practice behind my two Google certifications, labelled as practice throughout.

02Background

Search reaches people who are already looking; Meta finds people who aren't yet. Planning across both means reading search intent, keywords and on-site behaviour, not only ad-level metrics.

03Challenge

Learn Search and GA4 the way they're used on real accounts, not just well enough to pass the exams.

04Objective

  • Practise the full chain: intent, keywords, ads, landing page and measurement.
  • Read GA4 reports on realistic e-commerce data.
  • Plan a high-ticket funnel where lead quality matters more than volume.

05My role

Solo practice and self-study.

  • Built a Search campaign in a Google Ads simulation
  • Analysed a pre-filled e-commerce store's data in GA4
  • Worked through a 90-day strategy for a fictional high-ticket brief
  • Earned the Google Ads Search and Google Analytics (GA4) certifications

06Strategy

How I structure Search: one goal per campaign, ad groups split by intent, negatives to keep out the wrong searches, and a landing page that answers the search.

07Execution

The strategy exercise was a fictional brief, used as a learning exercise. It asked for a plan where a small sales team needs serious buyers, not more leads.

ScenarioHand-built premium electric motorcycles from €30,000, sold in Germany, Belgium, the Netherlands and France, with €10,000 a month across Google and Meta.
GoalsQualified test-ride bookings, serious buyer and dealer enquiries, a retargetable audience and an email list.
ConstraintA 3 to 6 month sales cycle, and a sales team that handles a few leads a week, so quality beats volume.
TasksA 90-day strategy and budget split; a Meta full-funnel plan with audiences and two ads; Google campaign types, 15+ keywords by intent, negatives, a responsive search ad and a landing page; a diagnosis of an underperforming funnel.

10Insights and learnings

Clicks are not the goal.

A 2.8% click-through rate looks healthy until only 0.2% of those clicks become leads. The planning chain is how I sanity-check any budget, on Search or Meta: cost per click, conversion rate, qualification and close rate decide whether it pays back. Next step: live Search campaigns.
