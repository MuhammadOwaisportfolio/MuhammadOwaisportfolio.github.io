01Project overview

A Meta sales strategy and video ad copy for a premium women's pret brand that sells stitched traditional suits online across Pakistan.

02Business background

The brand sits in the premium band, written for professional, upper-middle-class women, next to names like Beechtree, Azure, Maria B and Bonanza Satrangi. Its Meta ad account was new, with no sales history to learn from.

03Challenge

  • A fresh ad account with no data, so every early rupee buys learning.
  • A premium price that needs trust before anyone pays.
  • Cash-on-delivery orders, which need confirming before they ship.

04Objective

Find the creatives and audiences that sell, with clear rules for when to cut and when to scale, and keep every scaling decision above break-even.

05My role

  • Research: the brand's website, competitor sites and its Instagram content
  • The strategy document and the campaign structure
  • Testing and scaling rules
  • Creative briefs in four formats
  • Video ad copy: primary text, headline and description
  • A ranked list of cities to target across Pakistan

06Strategy

Test wide, decide by rule, scale slowly. Each ad set gets its own budget (ABO), so broad, interest and retargeting audiences are compared fairly.

07Execution

Creative briefs in four formats, each with minimal text and premium product shots, and premium-sounding video ad copy written for professional women.

08Tools and platforms

Meta Ads ManagerABO testingFacebook and InstagramWhatsApp BusinessCompetitor research

09Results

The client received the full strategy, the test plan, creative briefs and video ad copy. The first test window was short, so its numbers stay private: a few days is not a fair verdict for a premium product.

What the first test showed: high-price fashion needs a longer test window and retargeting before any verdict.

10Insights and learnings

Price changes the timeline.

A premium product needs more time and more touches before it converts. I set the test window and the break-even ROAS before launch, so a slow first week leads to a planned decision, not a panicked one.
